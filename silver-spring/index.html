Restoration Challenges Silver Spring Homeowners Face

Most water damage calls we take in Silver Spring trace back to a short list of causes. Knowing them makes it easier to catch trouble while it is still a small repair.

Pipes: A pipe that freezes can split, and the flooding starts once the ice thaws and pressure returns. Depending on the season, the plumbing most at risk runs through outside walls, attics and garages without heat. Corroded fittings and worn supply lines can also let go without any warning in older homes. Your first move is closing the main water valve, since a broken pipe floods room after room fast.

Appliances: The thin hoses feeding a washer, dishwasher or ice maker do not last forever. A water heater near the end of its life can leak from the tank bottom or fail all at once. We see many of these in Silver Spring homes where the unit sits upstairs or over a finished room.

Overflows: It takes only a few minutes for a blocked toilet or a forgotten faucet to flood a bathroom. The water often runs under baseboards and through the ceiling of the room below. If the overflow came from the toilet bowl or a drain, treat it as contaminated and keep children and pets away.

Roof: Rain gets in through worn flashing, damaged shingles or gutters that have stopped draining. By the time a brown ring appears overhead, the attic insulation has often been wet for a while. We dry the framing, insulation and drywall, and a roofer needs to fix the opening so it does not return.

Basement: Once the ground outside is soaked, water finds its way into a basement through wall cracks and the seam where floor meets wall. If the sump pump sticks or the electricity goes out mid-storm, the pit fills and spills across the slab. Finished basements suffer most, since carpet, drywall and framing all soak up water from the bottom.

Mold: Damp drywall, wood and carpet can begin to grow mold in as little as 24 to 48 hours. A lot of the mold we find in Silver Spring comes from a past leak that looked dry on the surface only. Have it looked at if you notice a stale odor, bubbling paint or dark specks around an earlier leak.
